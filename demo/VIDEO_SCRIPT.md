# CPR Coach: 3-minute video

Pre-recorded, edited, uploaded to YouTube. Nothing has to work live, so film each feature on its
own, keep the best take, and cut tight. Times are targets; the voiceover (VO) is about 380 words,
a comfortable pace for 3:00.

## What to film (shot list)

| # | Shot | How |
|---|------|-----|
| A | Wide: rescuer kneeling at a pillow/mannequin, phone propped on the floor beside them (side view), watch on the compressing wrist | Second phone on a tripod, landscape |
| B | Phone screen during each feature | iPhone Screen Recording (Control Center), **microphone ON** so the coach’s voice is captured |
| C | Watch close-up: cue text and the wrist tap | Second phone, handheld, close |
| D | Lock screen with the Live Activity | Screen Recording, press the side button mid-session |
| E | Slides as title cards | Present the deck full screen and screen-record, or export slides as images |

Edit B over A as picture-in-picture (phone screen in a corner, rescuer full frame) for the
compression shots. That one composition sells the product: a real person, real coaching.

**Filming switches built into the app**
- Long-press **Watch** in the top bar: off → recorded real session → recorded tiring rescuer → off.
  To film “Switch soon”: long-press twice (to the tiring session), then Start session. The
  warning appears about 40 seconds in and stays until depth drops below 5 cm (~55 s), when
  “Push harder” takes over. Speed up the wait in the edit.
- The emergency call never dials; it shows a demo banner.
- Hands-free voice may conflict with Screen Recording’s microphone. If commands aren’t heard,
  film the voice moments with shot A’s phone recording the room audio instead.

## Timeline

### 0:00–0:15 Hook
**Show:** slide *problem* (numbers), then a quick cut of someone collapsing (staged) and a bystander frozen.
**VO:** “Every year about 263,000 Americans have a cardiac arrest outside the hospital. Only about 4 in 10 get CPR from a bystander, and every minute without it cuts survival by up to 10 percent. Most of these happen at home, with family.”

### 0:15–0:25 The product
**Show:** shot A, the phone propped up, the watch on the wrist; then slide *product*.
**VO:** “CPR Coach turns the iPhone and Apple Watch you already own into a CPR instructor: the camera sees your form, the watch feels every push, and you talk to it hands-free.”

### 0:25–1:15 Real emergency, start to finish (the core)
1. **0:25** “Hey Siri, start CPR with CPR Coach.” (B) The emergency check opens.
2. **0:30** Coach asks “Are they responding?” Rescuer says “No.” “Breathing normally?” “No.” (B + room audio)
3. **0:38** Demo call banner, then compressions start with the metronome. (A + B picture-in-picture)
4. **0:45** Bend the elbows on purpose: “Lock your elbows.” Straighten: it goes quiet.
5. **0:52** Go slow: “Faster.” Speed up to the beat: rate turns green.
6. **0:58** “How am I doing?” Coach reads back rate and depth. (room audio)
7. **1:04** Watch close-up: cue text and the wrist tap. (C)
8. **1:08** Press the side button: lock screen Live Activity with rate, depth, instruction. (D)
9. **1:12** Tap **AED**: the pad guide opens.

**VO (sparse, over the action):** “It checks the person with you, gets help coming, and coaches every compression: elbows, rate, depth, all hands-free. The watch taps the beat and coaches on its own, and the session stays on your lock screen.”

### 1:15–1:45 The technology
**Show:** slide *fusion* (8 s), then B: depth tile reading “Watch + camera”; slide *predict* (6 s), then B: the “Switch soon” banner from the tiring-rescuer replay; recoil cue “Let the chest rise” if you got it on film.
**VO:** “Under the hood, a Kalman filter fuses watch acceleration with the camera’s view of your wrist for drift-free depth, and the camera calibrates itself from the watch. It predicts fatigue: it calls the swap before your compressions get shallow, not after. And it catches leaning on the chest, a mistake most people never notice.”

### 1:45–2:10 Built for everyone
**Show:** B: switch the language menu to Spanish; coach speaks Spanish. Child/infant picker. Then a training session’s summary with the on-device AI debrief, and a Skills check “Passed” card.
**VO:** “It works in seven languages, adapts for children and infants, and for practice it gives an on-device AI debrief and a two-minute skills check you can share. Video and voice never leave the phone.”

### 2:10–2:30 The handoff
**Show:** B: Stop, then **Report for paramedics**: timeline, stats, QR code.
**VO:** “When the ambulance arrives, paramedics get what they ask first: when the person was found, when help was called, how long CPR has run and how good it was.”

### 2:30–2:48 Proof
**Show:** slide *accuracy* with your numbers, or the validation report page (`make validate-report`).
**VO:** “We validated it against a click track and a fixed-depth stop: [__]% of compressions within 5 per minute, and [__]% within half a centimetre.”

### 2:48–3:00 Close
**Show:** slide *close*.
**VO:** “CPR Coach. The best CPR coach is the one already on your wrist.”

## Before filming
- Run the validation trials and fill in the numbers on the *accuracy* slide and in the VO.
- Watch app open and frontmost (Settings › General › Return to Clock › 1 hour on the watch).
- Do Not Disturb on both phones; status bar clean.
- A training session of at least 10 compressions before filming the debrief.
